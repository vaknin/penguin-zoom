Privacy Policy

Effective date: 15 November 2026 · Last updated: 15 November 2026

Short version. Penguin for Zoom keeps its settings on your computer. Your meeting audio, video, screen share and chat go directly between your computer and Zoom; we never see them. Our only server creates the join signature Zoom requires and keeps nothing about you after the request. No analytics, no cookies, no tracking, no ads, and we never sell or share your data.

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers the Penguin for Zoom desktop app, the signing service we run for it, this website, and our support channels.

It does not cover Zoom. When you join a meeting, Zoom processes your meeting data (audio, video, screen share, chat, name, and so on) under its own Privacy Statement and Terms of Service. The meeting host's settings (for example recording) are controlled by the host and Zoom, not by us.

4. Data the app gets from Zoom

Zoom requires apps like Penguin to join meetings as a signed-in Zoom user, so you sign in with your own Zoom account before joining. When you sign in, Zoom asks you to approve the app and gives it tokens for your account. The app asks Zoom only for:

  • A ZAK (scope user:read:zak): a short-lived token that lets the app join a meeting as you. It is fetched right before each join and handed back to Zoom.
  • Your basic profile (scope user:read:user): used only to show "Signed in as your name" in Settings. Zoom's answer also contains other profile fields (such as your email address and account ID); the app keeps only your first and last name, in memory, and discards the rest.

The app does not read your meetings list, contacts, recordings, chat history or any other Zoom data, and it does not change anything in your Zoom account.

Tokens are held only in the memory of the running app. They are not written to disk, so you sign in again after restarting the app. "Sign out" in Settings revokes the token with Zoom and clears it from memory.

5. Our signing service

Zoom requires every join to carry a signature made with the app's secret key. To keep that key off your computer, a small service we run on Oracle Cloud (over HTTPS) creates it. For each join it receives:

  • the meeting number and the role (participant or host), to put in the signature;
  • your IP address, as part of any internet connection; we use it only in memory to limit how often one address can ask for signatures;
  • only when you join as host: your Zoom access token, which the service sends to Zoom once to confirm you are signed in. It keeps a one-way hash of the token in memory for 5 minutes so it does not have to ask Zoom again for every request, and never keeps the token itself.

The service does not store meeting numbers, tokens or IP addresses, keeps no request logs that identify you, and has no database of users.

When you remove the app from your Zoom account, Zoom sends the service a deauthorization notice. Because we store nothing about you, there is nothing to delete; the service acknowledges the notice and discards it.

6. Why we use data, and legal bases

  • To let you join meetings (meeting number, role, display name, tokens, ZAK): needed to provide the app you asked to use (performance of a contract).
  • To protect the signing service from abuse (IP address in memory, token check): our legitimate interest in keeping the service available and secure.
  • To answer support requests (what you send us): to provide the service you asked for, and our legitimate interest in fixing problems.

We do not use any data for advertising, profiling, selling, or training machine-learning models, and we do not make automated decisions about you.

7. Who we share data with

  • Zoom Communications, Inc.: everything needed for the meeting goes to Zoom, as described above. See Zoom's Privacy Statement.
  • Oracle (Oracle Cloud Infrastructure): hosts our signing service and this website. Network traffic passes through Oracle's infrastructure; Oracle acts as our hosting provider. See Oracle's privacy policies.
  • GitHub, Inc.: hosts the source code, the package downloads and the issue tracker. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Our email provider, if you email us.
  • Authorities, only if the law requires it.

We do not sell personal information and do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are used in the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA).

8. How long data is kept

  • On your computer: settings until you change or delete them; the sign-in (refresh) token in your keyring until you sign out; other tokens and your profile name until you quit.
  • On our signing service: nothing after the request finishes, except the 5-minute in-memory token hash for host joins. A restart clears all memory.
  • Support: GitHub issues stay public until closed or deleted; emails are deleted once no longer needed, or on request.

9. After you remove the app

When you sign out, remove the app from your Zoom account, or uninstall it, we hold no data about you to delete: our signing service never stored any. Your settings file stays on your computer until you delete it. How to do each of these is in the documentation. Data that Zoom keeps about your meetings is handled by Zoom.

10. Security

All traffic to Zoom and to our signing service uses HTTPS. Sign-in uses OAuth with PKCE, so no app secret ships with the app. The app's local server only accepts requests from its own window. The sign-in token that keeps you signed in is stored in your system keyring (for example GNOME Keyring), never in a plain file; other tokens are kept in memory only, and all tokens are masked in diagnostic output. No method is perfectly secure; please report security problems privately to dibs.apps@gmail.com rather than in a public issue.

11. Your rights and how to use them

Depending on where you live (for example under the EU/UK GDPR, the Israeli Privacy Protection Law, or the CCPA/CPRA in California), you may have the right to:

  • know what personal information we hold about you and get a copy (access and portability);
  • have it corrected or deleted;
  • object to or restrict how we use it;
  • withdraw consent where we rely on it;
  • not be discriminated against for using these rights;
  • complain to a data protection authority (for example the Israeli Privacy Protection Authority, or your local authority in the EU or UK).

To make a request, email dibs.apps@gmail.com with the subject "Privacy request". We will answer within 30 days. Because the app keeps your data on your own computer and our service stores nothing, in most cases the answer is that we hold no data about you; you can delete the local data yourself as described in the documentation. For data Zoom holds, contact Zoom as described in its Privacy Statement.

12. International transfers

We are based in Israel. Our signing service runs in an Oracle Cloud data centre in Israel Central (Jerusalem), and GitHub's servers are mainly in the United States. Data that passes through these services may therefore be processed outside your country. Where the law requires, we rely on appropriate safeguards such as adequacy decisions or standard contractual clauses offered by these providers.

13. Children

Penguin is not directed to children under 16, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. Zoom's own age rules also apply to Zoom accounts. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

14. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top and keep the previous versions in the site's public history on GitHub. If a change affects what data we collect or how it is shared, we will also note it in the app's release notes before it takes effect.
